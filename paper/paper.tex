\begin{abstract}
Magic-state distillation is a major cost of fault-tolerant quantum computing. Any stabilizer protocol, however adaptive, postselected or multistage, can be replaced without loss of suppression order by a projection of its noisy T inputs onto one stabilizer code, and for targets with rational Pauli expectations a fault pattern is harmful if and only if its complement is. Factory design becomes an exact, finite problem. The eight-input quadratic CCZ factory is optimal, quadratic distillation of one T state needs exactly nine inputs, and rational magic targets need $n\geq2d+1$ at order $d$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Fault-tolerant quantum computers implement Clifford operations cheaply, but universal computation also requires non-Clifford resources. These are usually supplied as magic states, distilled from many noisy copies~\cite{bravyikitaev,bravyihaah}, and distillation is one of the dominant costs of fault tolerance. Magic-state cultivation~\cite{cultivation}, now demonstrated on hardware~\cite{googlecultivation}, prepares T states far more cheaply and may make large, high-order factories unnecessary. The error rates required by long algorithms, however, still call for small factories of low suppression order fed with cultivated states, and this is the regime in which the following question can be settled. Factory designs have improved steadily~\cite{campbellhoward,eastin,jones,haah,litinski,borrowed,jacinto,wjs,gong}, but how far they can improve is largely unknown. How many noisy T states must a protocol consume to output a target state with error $O(p^d)$, where $p$ is the input error rate and $d$ the \emph{suppression order}?

Existing lower bounds depend only weakly on $d$. Resource monotones that remain valid under postselection grow only like $1/p$ for a noisy T state, and so give no more than $n\gtrsim d$~\cite{fangliu,fangliu2,regulaprl}. Stronger monotone bounds assume deterministic or high-success-probability protocols~\cite{regulatakagi,seddon}, which practical factories are not. Exhaustive searches and classifications do resolve $d$, but only within particular code families or circuit layouts~\cite{nezamihaah,wjs,jacinto,borrowed,kbk}. Exact conversions without noise are better understood; for instance, six copies of an arbitrary pure magic qubit state are necessary and sufficient to make one CCZ state~\cite{rizzoleone}. Even for the CCZ state, whose eight-input quadratic factory~\cite{eastin,jones,haah} underlies many resource estimates~\cite{litinski,gidneyfowler}, it was not known whether an adaptive or multistage protocol could use fewer inputs.

In this Letter we show that, as far as the tolerance of input faults is concerned, adaptivity, randomness, postselection and multiple stages can be dispensed with. A set of fault patterns can be tolerated by some protocol if and only if some stabilizer code on the inputs projects each of them either onto one common vector, which encodes the target, or to zero. The reduction preserves the suppression order, although not the acceptance probability or the leading error coefficient. Moreover, flipping the sign of $\sqrt2$ commutes with stabilizer operations and fixes every target with rational Pauli expectations, CCZ among them, but maps a T state with error $p$ to one with error $1-p$. A fault pattern therefore damages the output if and only if its complement does. With these two facts, the minimum size of a factory becomes a finite and largely combinatorial problem.

We use the reductions in both directions [Fig.~\ref{fig:reductions}(d)]. Analytic arguments and exhaustive searches over all stabilizer codes give lower bounds, and the collapse test certifies factories proposed by integer and satisfiability programs, including two-level designs in which an outer code consumes CCZ states from small inner factories. Table~\ref{tab:final} collects the results. The eight-input quadratic CCZ factory is optimal among all stabilizer protocols, cubic and quartic CCZ need at least ten inputs, and rational targets that are not stabilizer states need $n\geq2d+1$. Quadratic distillation of one T state needs exactly nine inputs, one fewer than any previous protocol, and the search itself found the nine-input factory. Quadratic distillation of the controlled-S (CS) state needs exactly eight inputs, and a CCCZ state exactly six ideal T states. Because $\CS_{12}\CS_{13}|{+}{+}{+}\rangle$ is Clifford-equivalent to CCZ, a known 36-input cubic code~\cite{wjs} also distills CCZ, against 47 inputs reported so far, and a two-level quartic CCZ factory with \QuarticUB\ inputs has leading error $\QuarticCoef p^4$, against $2816p^4$ previously.

\section{Factories as codes}\label{sec:framework}
\paragraph*{Setting.}
Let $|T\rangle=(|0\rangle+\omega|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$ with $\omega=e^{i\pi/4}$. After the standard twirl~\cite{bravyikitaev}, each input is
\begin{equation}
 \rho_T(p)=(1-p)|T\rangle\langle T|+pZ|T\rangle\langle T|Z=\tfrac12\bigl[I+\tfrac{1-2p}{\sqrt2}(X+Y)\bigr].
 \label{eq:input}
\end{equation}
Clifford operations are assumed ideal, and we count the noisy inputs consumed per run. A fault pattern $e\in\F^n$ marks the faulty inputs; the states $|\phi_e\rangle=Z^e|T\rangle^{\otimes n}$ are orthonormal, and $\rho_T(p)^{\otimes n}=\sum_ew_e\rho_e$ with $\rho_e=|\phi_e\rangle\langle\phi_e|$ and $w_e=p^{|e|}(1-p)^{n-|e|}$. A \emph{protocol} applies stabilizer operations to the $n$ inputs: Clifford gates, stabilizer ancillas, Pauli measurements, classical feedforward and randomness, postselection and discarding, in any number of stages. Its accepted output is a trace-nonincreasing map $\Lambda$. For a pure target $\tau$ of $r$ qubits with projector $\Pi$, pattern $e$ has \emph{damage} $h_e=\tr[(I-\Pi)\Lambda(\rho_e)]\geq0$ and is \emph{harmless} if $h_e=0$, that is, if $\Lambda(\rho_e)\propto\Pi$. Since $\epsilon=\sum_ew_eh_e/\sum_ew_e\tr\Lambda(\rho_e)$, the protocol has suppression order $d$ [$\epsilon=O(p^d)$] exactly when it accepts the fault-free pattern and every pattern with $|e|<d$ is harmless. All statements concern individual patterns, so they hold for any fault distribution, correlated or not. We write $n_{\min}(\tau,d)$ for the least possible $n$; it is nondecreasing in $d$. The \emph{stabilizer nullity} $\nu(\tau)$ is $r$ minus the number of independent Pauli operators that fix $\tau$~\cite{beverland}; $\nu=3$ for CCZ. A target is \emph{rational} (\emph{dyadic}) if all its Pauli expectations are rational (of the form $k/2^j$). Phase states $2^{-r/2}\sum_xi^{f(x)}|x\rangle$ with integer $f$, such as CCZ, CS and CCCZ, are dyadic, whereas $|T\rangle$ is not rational.

\paragraph*{Reduction to a single code.}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:code}
Let $\nu(\tau)=r$, and let $H$ be a set of fault patterns containing $\mathbf 0$. Some protocol accepts the fault-free input and makes every pattern in $H$ harmless if and only if there is a stabilizer code $C$ on the $n$ inputs, with a Clifford encoding $U$ of $r$ qubits, such that $\Pi_C|\phi_{\mathbf0}\rangle\neq0$ and
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_C|\phi_e\rangle\in\mathbb C\,U|\tau\rangle\qquad\text{for every }e\in H.
 \label{eq:collapse}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
Figure~\ref{fig:reductions}(a,b) illustrates the statement. The proof (Sec.~\ref{app:proofs}) refines the protocol into branches given by single Kraus operators, as in the normal form of Campbell and Browne for single-output protocols~\cite{campbellbrowne}. Since damages are nonnegative, every branch is harmless on $H$, and each Kraus operator is a code projection followed by a Clifford isometry~\cite{fattal}. We call Eq.~\eqref{eq:collapse} \emph{collapse}: every tolerated input is projected onto one code vector or is rejected ($\Pi_C|\phi_e\rangle=0$). With the Gram matrix $G_{ef}=\langle\phi_e|\Pi_C|\phi_f\rangle$, and given that $\Pi_C|\phi_{\mathbf0}\rangle$ encodes $\tau$, collapse is the set of polynomial conditions $G_{\mathbf0\mathbf0}>0$ and $G_{\mathbf0\mathbf0}G_{ee}=|G_{\mathbf0e}|^2$ for $e\in H$; rejected patterns have $G_{ee}=G_{\mathbf0e}=0$. The theorem holds for any product of resource states, not only T states.

\paragraph*{Complementary faults.}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:pairing}
If $\tau$ is rational, then in every protocol a pattern $e$ is harmless if and only if its complement $e+\mathbf1$ is. With suppression order $d$, every pattern with $|e|<d$ or $|e|>n-d$ is harmless.
\end{theorem}
Every stabilizer operation maps Pauli coefficients by rational matrices and therefore commutes with the automorphism $\sigma$ of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ that flips the sign of $\sqrt2$. By Eq.~\eqref{eq:input}, $\sigma$ maps $\rho_T(p)$ to $\rho_T(1-p)$, hence $\rho_e$ to $\rho_{e+\mathbf1}$, and it fixes a rational target. Branch by branch, the damages of $e$ and of its complement are therefore conjugate in $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$, and one vanishes exactly when the other does (Sec.~\ref{app:proofs}). Beverland \emph{et al.} observed that $\sigma$ commutes with postselected stabilizer circuits and maps $|T\rangle$ to $Z|T\rangle$~\cite{beverland}; applied to noisy inputs, the same automorphism pairs each fault pattern with its complement. This accounts, for instance, for the invariance of Eastin's exact error polynomial for the CCZ factory under $p\mapsto1-p$~\cite{eastin}. In the code picture, extend $\sigma$ to amplitudes in $\mathbb Q(\omega)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,i)$ by fixing $i$. Then $\sigma(|T\rangle)=-Z|T\rangle$ and, since $\Pi_C$ has entries in $\mathbb Q(i)$, $\sigma(\Pi_C|\phi_{\mathbf0}\rangle)=(-1)^n\Pi_C|\phi_{\mathbf1}\rangle\neq0$. The fault-free and all-faulty inputs must therefore collapse together, and the projected ideal input is proportional to its own image under $\sigma$. We call this the \emph{cross condition} [Fig.~\ref{fig:reductions}(c)].

\paragraph*{A worked example.}
Three T states make the state $\CS|{+}{+}\rangle$: measure $Z_1Z_2Z_3$, keep the outcome $+1$ and decode. The code is the $+1$ eigenspace of $Z_1Z_2Z_3$, and the fault-free input projects onto the encoded target with probability $1/2$. The all-faulty input collapses onto the same vector, because $Z^{\otimes3}$ is itself the stabilizer, as Theorem~\ref{thm:pairing} requires. A single fault, by contrast, acts as a logical Pauli operator and is harmful. The protocol therefore has suppression order one, with output error exactly $3p(1-p)$, which is invariant under $p\mapsto1-p$. Section~\ref{app:example} gives every step explicitly and applies the same test to a seven-input code that the search rejects and to the eight-input CCZ factory, which passes.

\paragraph*{Targets up to Clifford equivalence.}
Appending a stabilizer branch that maps $\tau$ to $\tau'$ with nonzero probability turns every order-$d$ factory for $\tau$ into one for $\tau'$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:post}). In particular, $n_{\min}$ depends only on the Clifford orbit of the target, which is larger than the CNOT+S classes used to organize code families~\cite{wjs}:
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:orbit}
\begin{equation}
 \CS_{12}\CS_{13}|{+}{+}{+}\rangle=\mathrm{CX}_{23}H_2\mathrm{CX}_{12}S_2H_2|\CCZ\rangle,
 \label{eq:orbit}
\end{equation}
with the rightmost gate acting first.
\end{proposition}

\section{Lower bounds}\label{sec:lower}
\paragraph*{Analytic bounds.}
When $n$ is small, Theorem~\ref{thm:pairing} forces almost every pattern to be harmless. Feeding the protocol a suitably chosen state, none of whose fault patterns lies in the damaging range, then makes the target from too weak a resource (Sec.~\ref{app:analytic}). The witnesses are $|0\rangle^{\otimes n}$, a GHZ-type stabilizer state that contains every fault weight except the middle one, and a family of low-nullity states with non-dyadic Pauli expectations.
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:analytic}
Every protocol of suppression order $d$ satisfies $n\geq d-1+L(\tau)$, where $L(\tau)$ is the least number of ideal T states from which $\tau$ can be made with nonzero probability. If $\tau$ is rational and not a stabilizer state, then $L(\tau)\geq\nu(\tau)+1$, so that $n\geq d+\nu(\tau)$, and $n\geq2d+1$. If $\tau$ is dyadic and not a stabilizer state, and $d\geq2$, then $n\geq d+\nu(\tau)+1$.
\end{theorem}
The bound $L(\tau)\geq\nu(\tau)+1$ is the equality case of the canonical form of Beverland \emph{et al.} for postselected stabilizer circuits~\cite{beverland}; the bounds $2d+1$ and $d+\nu+1$ rest on Theorem~\ref{thm:pairing}. Linear growth in $d$ is the correct scaling: asymptotically good codes with transversal CCZ~\cite{golowich,nguyen,whyamasaki}, fed with four-T CCZ preparations, give one CCZ state with $n=O(d)$. The constant, however, is far from tight.

\paragraph*{Exhaustive search.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:code} reduces the existence of a protocol to a search over signed stabilizer codes. We parametrize each code by the X parts of its stabilizers, its Z-type stabilizers and the lifts, and reduce modulo input permutations and the Clifford $W=(X+Y)/\sqrt2$, which fixes $|T\rangle$. A simple necessary condition prunes most codes: no harmless $Z^e$ may act as a nontrivial logical operator (Lemma~\ref{lem:logical}). The remaining collapse identities are tested in exact arithmetic over $\mathbb Z[\sqrt2,i]$. Section~\ref{app:search} describes the search, its validation by an independent implementation and a compiled kernel, the size of every run (up to $9\times10^{11}$ signed codes) and the verification behind each result.
\begin{theorem}[computer-assisted]\label{thm:ccz}
Let $\tau$ be a 3-qubit state fixed by no Pauli operator other than the identity, for example CCZ. No stabilizer protocol has suppression order two with $n\leq7$ inputs. If $\tau$ is also rational, none has suppression order three, and hence none has order four, with $n\leq9$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{theorem}[computer-assisted]\label{thm:tcs}
No stabilizer protocol distills one T state with suppression order two from $n\leq\TNmax$ inputs. None distills $\CS|{+}{+}\rangle$ with order two from $n\leq7$ inputs, or with order three from $n\leq8$. Two CCZ states need eight ideal T states and, for quadratic suppression, ten noisy ones. The CCCZ state needs six ideal T states.
\end{theorem}
No protocol, single- or multistage, adaptive or not, therefore uses fewer inputs than the eight-input quadratic CCZ factory, and among codes of X-rank at most four that factory is unique up to symmetry. At $n=7$ the weight-one collapse conditions alone exclude every code, which is why the quadratic statement needs no rationality. The complementary-fault symmetry underlies the analytic bounds and, by enlarging $H$, makes the cubic searches feasible. For one T state, which is not rational, the search uses only patterns of weight below $d$. At $n=8$ it finds codes that collapse, but none of them encodes a state Clifford-equivalent to $|T\rangle$; most encode stabilizer states (Sec.~\ref{app:search}). At $n=9$ it finds a T-state factory, so the bound is tight (Proposition~\ref{prop:t9}).

\section{Constructions}\label{sec:upper}
We certify the suppression order of every construction below by the collapse test of Theorem~\ref{thm:code}, in exact arithmetic.

\paragraph*{A nine-input T factory from the search.}
A code that the exhaustive search finds to collapse onto an encoded target is itself a factory. For one T state at $n=9$ and $d=2$, the sectors of X-rank at most six contain 96 such codes, all equivalent to the following one.
\begin{proposition}\label{prop:t9}
Take nine noisy T states, measure the eight stabilizers
\begin{equation*}
\begin{array}{ll}
-\mathrm{XZIIZIIIX}, & +\mathrm{XIZIZIIXI},\\
+\mathrm{ZXZZIIXZI}, & +\mathrm{ZZXIZXIIZ},\\
-\mathrm{YXYIXIIZZ}, & -\mathrm{YYXXIIIII},\\
+\mathrm{IZIZZIZII}, & +\mathrm{IIZZZZIII},
\end{array}
\end{equation*}
and postselect on all outcomes $+1$. This succeeds with probability $1/128$ at $p=0$, and after a Clifford decoding the output is $|T\rangle$ with error $\epsilon=\tfrac{27}{2}p^2+O(p^3)$. Hence $n_{\min}(T,2)=9$.
\end{proposition}
An exact certificate (Sec.~\ref{app:search}) confirms that all nine single faults collapse and that the encoded state has logical Pauli expectations of moduli $\{0,1/\sqrt2,1/\sqrt2\}$, as $|T\rangle$ does. The best previous quadratic protocol uses ten inputs~\cite{mek}. The new code is not of generalized triorthogonal form: in that form, nine columns and two Z-type stabilizers would mean six check rows, and the integer program below shows that no matrix with nine columns and at most six check rows gives a quadratic T factory.

\paragraph*{Clifford orbits: a 36-input cubic CCZ factory.}
Many factories are built from a binary matrix $G$ with one column per input and a set of logical and check rows~\cite{bravyihaah,haah,wjs}. They implement a diagonal gate $U_\tau$ of the third level of the Clifford hierarchy on the logical register. When every overlap of rows that involves a check row is even (\emph{generalized triorthogonality}), they detect every fault pattern $e$ with $G_0e\neq0$, where $G_0$ consists of the check rows. All these conditions are parities of products of binary variables, so finding $G$ is an integer program (Sec.~\ref{app:programs}). The classification of Wills, Jain and Singh~\cite{wjs} identifies outputs only up to CNOT and S gates, and its CCZ class needs 47 inputs at $d=3$. By Proposition~\ref{prop:orbit}, however, its $\CS_{12}\CS_{13}$ class also yields CCZ states, and that class reaches distance three with 36 inputs. The saving reflects the T-count of the diagonal gate: four for $\CS_{12}\CS_{13}$, against seven for CCZ. Our integer program finds such a matrix independently, and the exact test certifies order three with leading error $120p^3$, as listed for that class in Ref.~\cite{wjs}.

\paragraph*{Stabilizer post-processing.}
Measuring one qubit of a CCZ state in the Y basis leaves $\CS|{+}{+}\rangle$ up to a Clifford correction (Proposition~\ref{prop:cs}). The eight-input CCZ factory followed by this measurement is therefore a quadratic CS factory with eight inputs, matching Theorem~\ref{thm:tcs}, whereas no generalized triorthogonal matrix with $n\leq10$ and at most three check rows achieves this. Similarly, projecting $|T\rangle^{\otimes6}$ onto the $[[6,4,2]]$ code with $Z^{\otimes6}=+1$ and $X^{\otimes6}=-1$ yields $X^{\otimes4}|\CCCZ\rangle$ up to Clifford phases, with probability $1/4$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:cccz}).

\paragraph*{Two-level factories.}
Within generalized triorthogonal codes, none beats 36 inputs for cubic or 48 for quartic CCZ~\cite{wjs}. Since any composition of stabilizer operations is again a protocol, an outer code may consume, besides raw T states, CCZ states produced by small inner factories. We use two inner factories: Jones' four-T preparation, which a single fault spoils, and the eight-T cube factory, which only two faults spoil. Each block applies a CCZ gate to three parities of the outer registers by gate teleportation, and a faulty block, after a Clifford twirl, applies a Z error to some nonzero combination of those parities. The composite has suppression order $D$ if every combination of faulty units whose total cost (one for a T input or Jones block, two for a cube block) is below $D$ is detected or harmless. All conditions remain parities, now including the cubic terms contributed by the blocks, and a SAT encoding solves these instances orders of magnitude faster than the integer program (Sec.~\ref{app:programs}). Six cube blocks give a quartic CCZ factory with \QuarticUB\ T inputs. Only 54 of the cost-four combinations, each a pair of faulty blocks, are harmful, and each arises from $4\times4$ pairs of input fault pairs. Hence $\epsilon=\QuarticCoef p^4+O(p^5)$, less than a third of the leading error of the best previous design with the same number of inputs. Five cube blocks (40 inputs), and mixtures of four cube blocks with Jones blocks, remain undecided after 15 minutes per instance, so it is open whether the two-level model admits a quartic factory with 40 inputs.

\section{Discussion}
The single-code reduction is restrictive enough to show that the eight-input CCZ factory is optimal and to give, to our knowledge, the first lower bounds beyond $\max(d,\nu)$ that hold under arbitrary postselection. Constructively, it certifies factories from Clifford orbits of code families and from two-level designs, and the exhaustive search produced the nine-input T factory, which lies outside every structured family that we modeled with integer programs. The remaining gaps in Table~\ref{tab:final} are $10\leq n\leq36$ and $10\leq n\leq\QuarticUB$ for cubic and quartic CCZ, $10\leq n\leq14$ for two CCZ states, $9\leq n\leq35$ for cubic CS, and $\TLB\leq n\leq15$ for cubic distillation of one T state.

Cultivation~\cite{cultivation,googlecultivation} moves the practical emphasis to low suppression orders, since a cultivated T state of small error needs only quadratic suppression to reach far lower errors. This is the regime that our results settle exactly, with eight inputs for CCZ, eight for CS and nine for one T state. Two limitations should be noted. The bounds count noisy inputs per run under ideal Clifford operations, not the space-time volume of a factory; and the nine-input T factory accepts only one run in 128, so per accepted output it consumes more inputs than the ten-input protocol.

The next exhaustive cases are limited by computing time alone. Excluding nine inputs for cubic distillation of one T state requires about $6\times10^{13}$ signed codes, for cubic CS about $10^{13}$, and excluding ten inputs for cubic and quartic CCZ about $2\times10^{15}$. At the measured rate of $7.5\times10^6$ signed codes per core-second, these runs take from hours to days on a thousand cores, and each subsequent step is roughly a thousand times larger.

Three further steps appear feasible. First, for CCZ at $n=8$ and $d=2$, all 137 collapsing codes of X-rank at most four contain $Z^{\otimes n}$ in their stabilizer group. A proof that this holds in general would eliminate the largest sector of the search and extend the exact bounds beyond ten inputs. Second, the collapse identities are polynomial, and relaxations of them, rather than of the set of protocols, could improve the analytic constant two. Third, the two-level model is a small, exact design space suited to SAT solvers; enlarging it with further inner factories, and with outputs taken up to Clifford equivalence, offers a systematic search for smaller factories.

\section{A worked example}\label{app:example}
We follow one small protocol through every step of the method, then apply the same test to a code that the exhaustive search rejects and to the eight-input CCZ factory. The script \texttt{worked\_example.py} reproduces every number below in exact arithmetic.

\paragraph*{Step 1: the protocol.}
Take three noisy T states. Measure $Z_1Z_2Z_3$ and keep the outcome $+1$. Then apply CNOT gates from qubits 1 and 2 onto qubit 3, which leaves qubit 3 in $|0\rangle$, and discard it. We show below that the output is $\CS|{+}{+}\rangle$ up to Clifford gates, with $\CS=\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,i)$.

\paragraph*{Step 2: the code.}
In the language of Theorem~\ref{thm:code}, the protocol projects onto the code $C$ fixed by $Z_1Z_2Z_3$, with projector $\Pi_C=(I+Z_1Z_2Z_3)/2$. It is spanned by the even-weight strings $|000\rangle,|011\rangle,|101\rangle,|110\rangle$ and has dimension $4=2^r$ with $r=2$. The decoder is the inverse of the Clifford encoding $U|y_1y_2\rangle=|y_1,y_2,y_1\oplus y_2\rangle$.

\paragraph*{Step 3: the fault-free input.}
Since $|T\rangle^{\otimes3}=2^{-3/2}\sum_x\omega^{|x|}|x\rangle$ and $\omega^2=i$, the projection keeps the even strings,
\begin{equation}
 \Pi_C|\phi_{\mathbf0}\rangle=2^{-3/2}\bigl(|000\rangle+i|011\rangle+i|101\rangle+i|110\rangle\bigr).
\end{equation}
Decoded, the amplitudes on $|y_1y_2\rangle$ are $(1,i,i,i)/2^{3/2}$, that is, $i^{y_1+y_2}(-i)^{y_1y_2}$ up to normalization. This is $(S\otimes S)\,\CS^{-1}|{+}{+}\rangle$, and $\CS^{-1}=\CS\cdot\mathrm{CZ}$, so the output is $\CS|{+}{+}\rangle$ up to Clifford gates. The Gram entry $G_{\mathbf0\mathbf0}=\|\Pi_C\phi_{\mathbf0}\|^2=1/2$ is the probability of accepting the ideal input.

\paragraph*{Step 4: the faulty inputs.}
Every $Z^e$ commutes with $Z_1Z_2Z_3$, so $\Pi_C|\phi_e\rangle=Z^e\,\Pi_C|\phi_{\mathbf0}\rangle$, and $G_{ee}=1/2$ for all $e$. Two cases occur.
\begin{itemize}
\item $e=111$. Here $Z^e=Z_1Z_2Z_3$ is the stabilizer itself, so $\Pi_C|\phi_{111}\rangle=\Pi_C|\phi_{\mathbf0}\rangle$: the all-faulty input collapses onto the fault-free one. In Gram form, $G_{\mathbf0\mathbf0}G_{ee}-|G_{\mathbf0e}|^2=\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac12-\tfrac14=0$. This is the cross condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:pairing} in its simplest form.
\item $e=100$, and likewise every pattern of weight one or two. Now $Z^e$ commutes with the stabilizer without being one, so it acts on $C$ as a logical Pauli operator; for $e=100$ it is $Z$ on the first logical qubit. The output becomes $Z_1\CS|{+}{+}\rangle$, which differs from the target. Indeed $G_{\mathbf0e}=\tfrac18(1+1-1-1)=0$, so the minor is $\tfrac14\neq0$. Lemma~\ref{lem:logical} predicts this without computation.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph*{Step 5: the error.}
Every pattern is accepted with probability $1/2$, and only $000$ and $111$ are harmless, so
\begin{equation}
 \epsilon(p)=1-(1-p)^3-p^3=3p(1-p).
\end{equation}
The suppression order is one, and the error is invariant under $p\mapsto1-p$, as Theorem~\ref{thm:pairing} demands. Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic} shows that suppression order two needs at least five inputs, and Theorem~\ref{thm:tcs} raises this to eight.

\paragraph*{Step 6: a code rejected by the search.}
For quadratic CCZ with seven inputs, the tolerated set $H$ contains the fault-free pattern, the seven single faults, their seven complements and the all-faulty pattern. The first code of X-rank one that survives the pruning of Lemma~\ref{lem:logical} has stabilizers
\begin{equation}
 X_1X_2X_3X_4X_5X_6,\quad Z_7,\quad Z_1Z_6,\quad Z_2Z_3Z_4Z_5.
\end{equation}
It passes the pruning because every tolerated $Z^e$ that commutes with all stabilizers is itself a stabilizer: $Z_7$, $Z_1\cdots Z_6=(Z_1Z_6)(Z_2Z_3Z_4Z_5)$ and $Z^{\otimes7}$. Every other single fault is detected. With all signs $+1$, the exact Gram entries are $G_{\mathbf0\mathbf0}=1/16$ and, for a fault on input 1, $G_{\mathbf0e}=-i/32$ and $G_{ee}=1/16$. Hence
\begin{equation}
 G_{\mathbf0\mathbf0}G_{ee}-|G_{\mathbf0e}|^2=\tfrac1{256}-\tfrac1{1024}=\tfrac3{1024}\neq0 .
\end{equation}
The faulty input projects partly along the ideal vector and partly orthogonally to it, so it produces a wrong output state. Each of the 16 sign choices fails twelve of the fifteen conditions. The search runs this test on all 7{,}699{,}696 signed codes that survive the pruning, and none passes.

\paragraph*{Step 7: the eight-input factory passes.}
Label the eight inputs by the vertices $v\in\F^3$ of a cube. Writing the T$^\dagger$ inputs of the usual construction as $S^\dagger|T\rangle$, the stabilizers are $X$ on the four even-weight vertices times $Y$ on the four odd-weight vertices, together with $Z$ on the faces $v_1=0$, $v_2=0$, $v_3=0$ and $v_1=1$. These generate $Z$ on all six faces and $Z^{\otimes8}$. The test gives the following.
\begin{itemize}
\item $G_{\mathbf0\mathbf0}=1/16$, and $\Pi_C\phi_{\mathbf0}$ has the Pauli spectrum of CCZ.
\item Every single fault anticommutes with the first stabilizer and is rejected: $G_{ee}=G_{\mathbf0e}=0$.
\item Since $Z^{\otimes8}$ is a stabilizer, each pattern behaves like its complement. The weight-seven patterns are rejected, and the all-faulty input collapses onto the fault-free one, with $G_{\mathbf0\mathbf1}=G_{\mathbf1\mathbf1}=1/16$.
\item Every $Z_jZ_k$ commutes with all stabilizers but is not one, so it is a logical error. All 28 weight-two patterns are harmful, and $\epsilon=28p^2+O(p^3)$.
\end{itemize}
So eight inputs reach order two, while the search shows that seven never do.

\section{Proofs of the reductions}\label{app:proofs}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:code}]
Write the accepted map as $\Lambda=\sum_bc_b\Lambda_b$ over branches, where a branch fixes all random choices and measurement outcomes. Each branch damage $h_{b,e}=\tr[(I-\Pi)\Lambda_b(\rho_e)]$ is nonnegative, and these damages sum to the damage of $e$, so every branch is harmless on $H$. Measuring each discarded qubit in the computational basis refines the branches into branches given by single Kraus operators $K$. Pick one with $K|\phi_{\mathbf0}\rangle\neq0$, which exists because the fault-free input is accepted. $K$ is a product of Clifford gates, stabilizer preparations and Pauli projections~\cite{campbellbrowne}, so applied to half of $n$ Bell pairs it yields a pure stabilizer state on $n+r$ qubits. Every bipartite pure stabilizer state equals, up to local Clifford gates, a product of Bell pairs and single-qubit states~\cite{fattal}. Hence $K=c\,V\Pi_C$, where $C$ is a stabilizer code on the inputs and $V$ a Clifford isometry from $C$ onto its range. Since $K|\phi_{\mathbf0}\rangle\propto|\tau\rangle$ and $\tau$ is fixed by no Pauli operator other than the identity, the range is the whole output space, so $\dim C=2^r$ and $U=V^{-1}$ is an encoding. Harmlessness of $e$ in this branch is Eq.~\eqref{eq:collapse}. Conversely, measuring the stabilizers of $C$, postselecting the prescribed signs and applying $V$ is a protocol. For the Gram form, $G_{\mathbf0\mathbf0}>0$ is $\Pi_C|\phi_{\mathbf0}\rangle\neq0$, and given this, $\Pi_C|\phi_e\rangle\in\mathbb C\,\Pi_C|\phi_{\mathbf0}\rangle$ (including $\Pi_C|\phi_e\rangle=0$) is equality in the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality $|G_{\mathbf0e}|^2\leq G_{\mathbf0\mathbf0}G_{ee}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:pairing}]
Let $\sigma$ flip the sign of $\sqrt2$ in every Pauli coefficient of the form $x+y\sqrt2$ with $x,y$ rational. Each step of a branch maps Pauli coefficients by rational matrices. Cliffords permute Paulis up to sign, stabilizer preparations have rational coefficients, partial traces multiply by integers, and a measurement outcome acts as $O\mapsto(I\pm P)O(I\pm P)/4$. Hence $\sigma\circ\Lambda_b=\Lambda_b\circ\sigma$. By Eq.~\eqref{eq:input}, $\sigma(\rho_T(p))=\rho_T(1-p)$, so $\sigma(\rho_e)=\rho_{e+\mathbf1}$, and $\sigma$ fixes the projector onto a rational $\tau$. Thus $\sigma(h_{b,e})=h_{b,e+\mathbf1}$, and $\sigma$ maps only zero to zero. For the cross condition of the main text, extend $\sigma$ to $\mathbb Q(\omega)$ by $\sigma(i)=i$, so that $\sigma(\omega)=-\omega$ and $\sigma(|T\rangle)=-Z|T\rangle$, hence $\sigma(|\phi_e\rangle)=(-1)^n|\phi_{e+\mathbf1}\rangle$ entrywise. Since $\Pi_C$ has entries in $\mathbb Q(i)$, $\sigma(\Pi_C|\phi_{\mathbf0}\rangle)=(-1)^n\Pi_C|\phi_{\mathbf1}\rangle$, which is nonzero. As $\mathbf1$ is harmless, Eq.~\eqref{eq:collapse} makes this vector proportional to $\Pi_C|\phi_{\mathbf0}\rangle$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[No hidden logical]\label{lem:logical}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:code}, if $e\in H$ is nonzero and $Z^e$ commutes with all stabilizers of $C$, then $\pm Z^e$ is a stabilizer.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Otherwise $Z^e$ is a nontrivial logical operator with $\Pi_CZ^e\phi_{\mathbf0}=Z^e\Pi_C\phi_{\mathbf0}\propto\Pi_C\phi_{\mathbf0}$, so the encoded target would be fixed by a Pauli operator.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Post-processing]\label{lem:post}
If a stabilizer branch maps $|\tau\rangle$ to $|\tau'\rangle$ with nonzero probability, then $n_{\min}(\tau',d)\leq n_{\min}(\tau,d)$. This holds in particular for Clifford equivalence and for discarding a tensor factor.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Patterns that produced exactly $\tau$ now produce exactly $\tau'$, and the fault-free input is still accepted.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:orbit}]
Direct computation on the eight amplitudes, checked in exact arithmetic (supplementary code). A breadth-first search shows that the Clifford orbit of $|\CCZ\rangle$ has 69{,}120 elements up to phase. Its diagonal third-level members have no T terms. Up to S gates, their phase polynomials are $4x_1x_2x_3$ plus zero, one or three CS terms, or exactly two CS terms and no cubic term. These are the CNOT+S classes of CCZ and $\CS_{12}\CS_{13}$~\cite{wjs}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:cs}
Projecting the third qubit of $|\CCZ\rangle$ onto $(|0\rangle\pm i|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$ leaves $\CS^{\pm1}|{+}{+}\rangle$, so $n_{\min}(\CS,d)\leq n_{\min}(\CCZ,d)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The remaining amplitudes are $1\mp i(-1)^{x_1x_2}$, whose ratio between $x_1x_2=1$ and $x_1x_2=0$ is $\pm i$. Since $\CS^{-1}=\CS\cdot\mathrm{CZ}$, both outcomes give $\CS|{+}{+}\rangle$ up to a Clifford gate; apply Lemma~\ref{lem:post}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:cccz}
$L(\CCCZ)=6$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The search excludes every code on five inputs. For the matching construction, project $|T\rangle^{\otimes6}$ onto $Z^{\otimes6}=+1$ and $X^{\otimes6}=-1$; this succeeds with probability $1/4$. The code space is spanned by $|x\rangle-|\bar x\rangle$ for even-weight $x$. On the representatives $x=(y,y_1\oplus\dots\oplus y_4,0)$, $y\in\F^4$, the amplitudes are proportional to $(-1)^{e_3(y)+e_4(y)}$, where $e_k$ are the elementary symmetric polynomials. Since $e_4(y\oplus\mathbf1)=e_4+e_3+e_2+e_1+1$ modulo two, this is $X^{\otimes4}|\CCCZ\rangle$ up to the Clifford phases $(-1)^{e_1+e_2+1}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Analytic bounds}\label{app:analytic}
The Clifford $W=(X+Y)/\sqrt2$ fixes $|T\rangle$ and negates $Z|T\rangle$, so $W^a\phi_e=(-1)^{a\cdot e}\phi_e$. Applying $W$ to each qubit of $|\psi\rangle$ independently with probability one half removes all coherence between patterns:
\begin{equation}
 2^{-n}\textstyle\sum_aW^a|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|W^a=\sum_eq_e\rho_e,\qquad q_e=|\langle\phi_e|\psi\rangle|^2.
 \label{eq:twirl}
\end{equation}
We say that $\psi$ \emph{contains} the patterns with $q_e>0$.

\begin{lemma}[Witness]\label{lem:witness}
If $\psi$ contains the fault-free pattern and only harmless patterns, then a stabilizer branch maps $|\psi\rangle$ to $|\tau\rangle$ with nonzero probability.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Feed the mixture~\eqref{eq:twirl} to the protocol. The output $\sum_eq_ea_e\Pi$ is a positive multiple of the target. Splitting it by twirl choice and by branch writes a multiple of a pure state as a sum of positive operators. Each of them is then a multiple of $\Pi$, and at least one is nonzero.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Nullity]\label{lem:nullity}
If a stabilizer branch maps $|\psi\rangle$ to $|\varphi\rangle$, then $\nu(\psi)\geq\nu(\varphi)$, and at equality every Pauli expectation of $\psi$ is zero or $\pm$ one of $\varphi$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Postpone discarding to the end. Ancillas and Cliffords preserve the nullity. Consider a projection $(I+P)/2$ on a pure state $\chi$. If $\pm P\in S_\chi$, it acts trivially or annihilates $\chi$. If $P$ anticommutes with some $g\in S_\chi$, it acts as the Clifford $(P+g)/\sqrt2$. Otherwise it lowers the nullity by one. A pure output means the remaining qubits are in a state $\varphi\otimes\kappa$ of nullity $\nu(\varphi)+\nu(\kappa)$. At equality, no projection lowers the nullity and $\kappa$ is a stabilizer state, so $|\varphi\rangle|\kappa\rangle=C|\psi\rangle|s\rangle$ for a Clifford $C$, which maps Pauli expectations to Pauli expectations up to sign.
\end{proof}

Since $|0\rangle=(|T\rangle+Z|T\rangle)/\sqrt2$, the state $|0\rangle^{\otimes n}$ contains every pattern. For even $N$, the stabilizer state $|G_N\rangle=(|{+_i}\rangle^{\otimes N}-|{-_i}\rangle^{\otimes N})/\sqrt2$ has $\langle\phi_e|G_N\rangle=(\alpha^{N-|e|}\beta^{|e|}-\beta^{N-|e|}\alpha^{|e|})/\sqrt2$, with $\alpha=(1+\omega)/2$ and $\beta=(1-\omega)/2$. Since $|\alpha/\beta|=1+\sqrt2$, this vanishes exactly when $|e|=N/2$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic}, first two claims]
The state $|T\rangle^{\otimes(n-d+1)}|0\rangle^{\otimes(d-1)}$ contains only patterns of weight below $d$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:witness} gives $n-d+1\geq L(\tau)$. Let $\tau$ be rational and not a stabilizer state. Lemma~\ref{lem:nullity}, applied to a branch mapping $|T\rangle^{\otimes L}$ with $L=L(\tau)$ to $\tau$, gives $L\geq\nu(\tau)$. At equality every Pauli expectation of $|T\rangle^{\otimes L}$ would be zero or $\pm$ one of $\tau$, but $\langle X\rangle=1/\sqrt2$ is irrational; hence $L(\tau)\geq\nu(\tau)+1$ and $n\geq d+\nu(\tau)$. (Equivalently, at equality the canonical form of Ref.~\cite[Theorem~5.3]{beverland} contains no projections, and $\tau$ would be Clifford-equivalent to $|T\rangle^{\otimes L}$ up to stabilizer factors.) If $n<2d$, Theorem~\ref{thm:pairing} makes every pattern harmless, and $|0\rangle^{\otimes n}$ would turn a stabilizer state into $\tau$, which is impossible since $\nu(\tau)\geq1$. If $n=2d$, only patterns of weight $n/2$ can be harmful, and $G_n$ contains none of them.
\end{proof}

For the third claim, split the inputs into a block $A$ of $a\geq2$ qubits and a block $B$ of $d$ qubits. Set $|\beta\rangle=(1+\bar\omega)^d|{+}\rangle^{\otimes d}-(1-\bar\omega)^d|{-}\rangle^{\otimes d}$ and $|\psi\rangle\propto|T\rangle^{\otimes a}|\beta\rangle+(Z|T\rangle)^{\otimes a}Z^{\otimes d}|\beta\rangle$.
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:magicwitness}
The state $\psi$ has $\nu(\psi)\leq a$. It contains the fault-free pattern, and only patterns with fewer than $d$ or more than $a$ faults. For $d\geq2$, some Pauli expectation of $\psi$ is not dyadic.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The operators $X_jX_{j+1}$ on neighboring qubits of $B$ fix both terms, and $Z^{\otimes(a+d)}$ swaps them; these are $d$ independent stabilizers. Since $\langle T|Z|T\rangle=0$, the $A$ part of every contained pattern is all-clear or all-faulty. With $\langle T|{\pm}\rangle=(1\pm\bar\omega)/2$ and $\langle ZT|{\pm}\rangle=(1\mp\bar\omega)/2$, the all-faulty pattern of $B$ is absent from $\beta$, and the fault-free one is absent from $Z^{\otimes d}\beta$. The fault-free amplitude $[(1+\bar\omega)^{2d}-(1-\bar\omega)^{2d}]2^{-d}$ is nonzero. For $X$ on one qubit of each block, $\langle X_AX_B\rangle=[(2+\sqrt2)^d-(2-\sqrt2)^d]/\{\sqrt2[(2+\sqrt2)^d+(2-\sqrt2)^d]\}$. Writing $(1+\sqrt2)^d=P+Q\sqrt2$ with $P,Q$ positive integers, this equals $Q/P$ for even $d$ and $P/(2Q)$ for odd $d$. From $P^2-2Q^2=(-1)^d$, $P$ is odd and coprime to $Q$. For even $d\geq2$ the reduced denominator is $P\geq3$, which is odd. For odd $d\geq3$ it is $2Q$, and $Q=\sum_k\binom{d}{2k+1}2^k\equiv d\equiv1$ modulo two, with $Q\geq5$; so $Q$ is an odd factor greater than one. (At $d=1$, $P=Q=1$ and the value $1/2$ is dyadic.)
\end{proof}
\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic}, third claim]
If $\nu(\tau)\leq d$, the claim already follows from $n\geq2d+1$, so let $\nu=\nu(\tau)\geq d+1$ and suppose $n\leq d+\nu$. Since $n\geq2d+1$, the block size $a=n-d$ satisfies $3\leq d+1\leq a\leq\nu$. All patterns in $\psi$ are harmless by Theorem~\ref{thm:pairing}, so Lemma~\ref{lem:witness} maps $\psi$ to $\tau$. Lemma~\ref{lem:nullity} then forces $\nu(\psi)=\nu(\tau)$ and dyadic expectations, which contradicts Lemma~\ref{lem:magicwitness}.
\end{proof}

For CCZ these bounds give six inputs at $d=2$, whereas $n_{\min}(\CCZ,2)=8$; closing the gap requires the full collapse condition, which the exhaustive search of Sec.~\ref{app:search} tests.

\section{Exhaustive search}\label{app:search}
\paragraph*{Parametrization.}
For a code with $m=n-r$ generators, the X parts of the stabilizers form a subspace $A$ of dimension $a$, and the Z-type stabilizers $Z^b$ form a subspace $B\subseteq A^\perp$ of dimension $m-a$. A basis $x_i$ of $A$ has lifts $X^{x_i}Z^{z_i}$, unique modulo $B$, which commute if and only if $x_i\cdot z_j=x_j\cdot z_i$. All $2^m$ sign choices are handled at once by a Walsh--Hadamard transform over the group.

\paragraph*{Symmetry, pruning and arithmetic.}
$A$ is taken up to input permutations, as one canonical representative per orbit, given by its reduced echelon basis $x_1,\dots,x_a$. For each such $A$ we enumerate every $B\subseteq A^\perp$ and every lift, with no further reduction by the permutations that fix $A$. Lifts are reduced modulo $B$, so their bits at the pivots of $B$ vanish. The Clifford $W_j$ on input $j$ maps $X_j\mapsto Y_j$ and $Z_j\mapsto-Z_j$; it leaves $A$ and $B$ unchanged, adds $x_{i,j}$ to bit $j$ of every $z_i$, and, since $W^s\phi_e=(-1)^{s\cdot e}\phi_e$, preserves every $|G_{ef}|$. So for each input $j$ in the support of $A$ that is not a pivot of $B$ we fix $z_{i,j}=0$ for the first $i$ with $x_{i,j}=1$; inputs outside the support of $A$ carry no gauge. Lemma~\ref{lem:logical} removes every pair $(A,B)$ for which some nonzero $e\in H$ orthogonal to $A$ lies outside $B$. The Gram entries follow from $\langle\phi_e|i^pX^xZ^z|\phi_f\rangle=[k\subseteq x]\,2^{-|x|/2}\,i^{p+2|e\wedge x|-|k|}$, where $k=z\oplus e\oplus f$ is a sum in $\F^n$ and the exponent is an integer. After scaling they lie in $\mathbb Z[\sqrt2,i]$, and every minor is tested exactly.

\paragraph*{Fault sets.}
For rational targets (CCZ, CCZ$^{\otimes2}$, CS, CCCZ) every run takes $H=\{e:|e|<d\text{ or }|e|>n-d\}$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:pairing}, both in Lemma~\ref{lem:logical} and in the collapse test; at $d=1$ this is $\{\mathbf0,\mathbf1\}$. The runs labelled ``3-qubit, $\nu=3$'' and ``T'' in Table~\ref{tab:runs} use $H=\{e:|e|<d\}$ only.

\paragraph*{Target tests.}
Collapse does not see which state is encoded. Since the code space is $U(\mathbb C^{2^r}\otimes|0\rangle^{\otimes m})$ for a Clifford encoding $U$, the normalized $n$-qubit state $\Pi_C|\phi_{\mathbf0}\rangle/\sqrt{G_{\mathbf0\mathbf0}}$ must be Clifford-equivalent to $|\tau\rangle|0\rangle^{\otimes m}$, with the $m=n-r$ syndrome qubits in $|0\rangle$. The Python search keeps a survivor only if the multiset of absolute Pauli expectations of this normalized state equals that of $|\tau\rangle|0\rangle^{\otimes m}$, a necessary condition for Clifford equivalence. The compiled kernel tests collapse only.

For one T state at $n=8$ and $d=2$, with $H=\{e:|e|<2\}$, the search covers all X-ranks, $1.32\times10^{11}$ signed codes in total. The sectors of X-rank at most four (Python, with the target test) contain no T-state protocol, and the X-rank-seven sector ($1.2\times10^{11}$ signed codes, compiled kernel) has no collapse survivor. The X-rank-six sector ($1.1\times10^{10}$ signed codes) has 890{,}940 collapse survivors and the X-rank-five sector ($9.1\times10^{8}$) has 346{,}136, which we classified exactly. For each code we compute two logical Paulis by linear algebra over $\F$ and the three logical expectations of the normalized state $\Pi_C\phi_{\mathbf0}/\sqrt{G_{\mathbf0\mathbf0}}$. A single-qubit Clifford only permutes these up to sign, and for $|T\rangle$ their moduli are $\{1/\sqrt2,1/\sqrt2,0\}$. All survivors of X-rank six, and all but 928 of X-rank five, have one modulus equal to one and so encode stabilizer states. The remaining 928 have moduli $\{0,1/3,2\sqrt2/3\}$: they encode a non-stabilizer state, but not one in the Clifford orbit of $|T\rangle$. The classifier agrees with a full Pauli-spectrum computation on random samples and on all saved non-stabilizer survivors, and it recognizes a T-equivalent control.

At $n=9$ we ran the compiled kernel on the sectors of X-rank at most six ($2.2\times10^{11}$ signed codes) and classified their $2.5\times10^{7}$ collapse survivors in the same way. Almost all encode stabilizer states, a few thousand encode other non-stabilizer states, and 96 encode a state with the logical Bloch moduli of $|T\rangle$. These 96 codes all accept the ideal input with probability $1/128$ and have the same error polynomial, and they are equivalent to the code of Proposition~\ref{prop:t9}. For that code an independent certificate recomputes, in exact arithmetic over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,i)$, all single-fault minors, the logical Bloch vector and the full error polynomial: the harmful patterns number 36, 45, 90, 75, 76, 15, 6 and 1 at weights two to nine, and $\epsilon=\tfrac{27}{2}p^2+O(p^3)$. A dense computation confirms collapse and the Pauli spectrum of $|T\rangle$ for all 96 codes. The sectors of X-rank seven and eight were not run, since the factory already shows that nine inputs suffice.

\paragraph*{Padding and monotonicity.}
A protocol on $n-1$ inputs, extended by one discarded input, keeps its suppression order, so each search at fixed $n$ also excludes all smaller $n$. Order $d$ implies order $d-1$, so the quartic CCZ bound follows from the cubic one, and the quartic run is a consistency check.

\paragraph*{Validation.}
For $n\leq5$ the search agrees with brute force over all signed codes. Six hundred random codes at $n=6,7$ map under the symmetries to visited codes with identical Gram matrices, and exact and floating-point entries agree. The search recovers Jones' four-T CCZ preparation, the three-T CS preparation and the eight-T cube factory. A separately written implementation, without the $W$ symmetry and with its own arithmetic, confirms the CCZ runs at $n=7$. A compiled kernel runs the largest sectors and reproduces the Python search on every smaller case. The two-CCZ and CCCZ runs use the Python implementation only. Table~\ref{tab:runs} lists the decisive runs.

\section{Integer and SAT programs}\label{app:programs}
\paragraph*{Phase-polynomial factories.}
Registers $v=(y,c)$, with $k$ logical and $s$ check bits, start in $|{+}\rangle$. Each column $G_j$ applies T to the parity $G_j\cdot v$, consuming one input, so the phase is $\omega^{g(v)}$ with $g(v)=\sum_j[G_j\cdot v]$, where $[\cdot]\in\{0,1\}$ is a parity read as an integer. A faulty input $j$ applies $ZT$ instead of T to that parity, an extra sign $(-1)^{G_j\cdot v}$. These signs multiply, with no cross terms, so a fault pattern $e$ of any weight contributes $\prod_{j:e_j=1}(-1)^{G_j\cdot v}=(-1)^{(Ge)\cdot v}$. Suppose every overlap $\sum_jG_{aj}G_{bj}G_{cj}$ (rows not necessarily distinct) that involves a check row is even. Then the check-involving part $P(y,c)=g(y,c)-g(y,0)$ has even linear, doubly even quadratic and no cubic terms modulo eight, so $\omega^{-P}$ is a diagonal Clifford. Undoing it and projecting the checks onto $|{+}\rangle^{\otimes s}$ gives, on an input fault $e$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{out}_e(y)=\textstyle\sum_c\omega^{g(y,c)-P(y,c)}(-1)^{(Ge)\cdot(y,c)},
 \label{eq:out}
\end{equation}
which is one branch of a stabilizer protocol.

\paragraph*{Integer program.}
The variables are the entries $G_{aj}\in\{0,1\}$. For every set $R$ of at most three rows and every column $j$, a binary variable $y_{R,j}$ represents the product $\prod_{a\in R}G_{aj}$ through the standard linearization $y_{R,j}\leq G_{aj}$ for $a\in R$ and $y_{R,j}\geq\sum_{a\in R}G_{aj}-|R|+1$. For every $R$, an integer slack $t_R$ enforces $\sum_jy_{R,j}=2t_R+\tau_R$, where $\tau_R$ is zero if $R$ contains a check row and is otherwise the target's T, CS or CCZ coefficient. Every column is nonzero. Distance two requires every column, including check-only columns, to have a nonzero check part, and distance three requires the check parts of all columns to be pairwise distinct, so that no fault of weight one or two has $G_0e=0$. To break the column-permutation symmetry, the columns are sorted in nondecreasing order of their binary value or, at distance three, in strictly increasing order of the binary value of their check parts, which also makes the check parts distinct. A run covers the target's whole CNOT+S class, and we run every class in its Clifford orbit. Gurobi~12 finds the cube factory and Bravyi and Kitaev's 15-to-1 protocol within seconds. It also proves in-class infeasibility in two cases used in the main text: one T state at $d=2$ for $n\leq9$ with at most six check rows, and CS at $d=2$ for $n\leq10$ with at most three check rows. These in-class results are not used in Table~\ref{tab:final}.

\paragraph*{Certification.}
For every design we check exactly that $\omega^{-P}$ is Clifford, and evaluate Eq.~\eqref{eq:out} in $\mathbb Z[\omega]$ for every fault pattern below the target order. We test collapse through all $2\times2$ minors, count the harmful patterns at the target order, and compare the Pauli spectrum of the output with that of the target. The cube factory and 15-to-1 give $28p^2$ and $35p^3$.

\paragraph*{Two-level model.}
Besides T columns, the outer code may contain CCZ blocks $(a,b,c)$ of three parity vectors. A block is a CCZ state from an inner factory, consumed by gate teleportation, and it multiplies the outer state by $(-1)^{[a\cdot v][b\cdot v][c\cdot v]}$. Modulo eight this phase is $4\,(a\cdot v)(b\cdot v)(c\cdot v)$. Its quadratic and linear parts are CZ and Z gates. Its cubic part adds to every distinct triple of rows the symmetrized tensor $\sum_\pi a_{\pi_1}b_{\pi_2}c_{\pi_3}$ modulo two. The twirl by the CCZ-preserving Cliffords $X_i\mathrm{CZ}_{jk}$ turns any error of a block into a mixture of $Z^l$ with $l\in\F^3\setminus\{0\}$, which acts on the outer state as $Z^{l_1a+l_2b+l_3c}$. A Jones block (four T, inner order one) has fault cost one; a cube block (eight T, inner order two) has fault cost two. Suppose every combination of faulty units (T columns or blocks, with any patterns) of total cost below $D$ has a nonzero check part or a zero logical part. Then fewer than $D$ input faults are always harmless, and by Theorem~\ref{thm:code} and Lemma~\ref{lem:post}, applied stage by stage, the composite has order $D$. The CNF encoding uses Tseitin variables for products and XOR chains for parities. CaDiCaL~1.9.5 solves the instances, and each satisfying design is certified exactly as above. \section{Prior lower bounds in our setting}\label{app:prior}
\paragraph*{Valid under arbitrary postselection.}
Fang and Liu~\cite{fangliu,fangliu2} give $n\geq\log[(1-f_\tau)/\epsilon]/\log[(1+R)/\lambda_{\min}]$, with stabilizer fidelity $f_\tau$ of the target, generalized robustness $R$ of the input (the least $s$ such that $(\rho+s\sigma)/(1+s)$ is a stabilizer mixture for some state $\sigma$) and smallest input eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}$. Their statement also contains a success-probability factor, which can be removed~\cite{regulaprl}. For $\rho_T(p)$, $\lambda_{\min}=p$, and by convexity $R(\rho_T(p))\leq R(|T\rangle)=3-2\sqrt2$: the Bloch vector $(1,1,0)/\sqrt2$ must be mixed with its antipode to reach the stabilizer octahedron $|x|+|y|+|z|\leq1$. Thus $1+R\leq4-2\sqrt2$, which coincides with the inverse stabilizer fidelity of $|T\rangle$, and the bound tends to $d$ as $p\to0$. (With the standard robustness, $1+R(|T\rangle)=(1+\sqrt2)/2$, the limit and the integer values below are unchanged.) At $p=10^{-3}$, with the constants of known protocols, it gives $n\geq3$ for T at $d=3$ and $n\geq2$ for CCZ at $d=2$. Regula's projective robustness~\cite{regulaprl} grows like $1/p$ and has the same limit. The nullity gives $n\geq\nu$ at every $d\geq1$, since $\epsilon(0)=0$ forces an exact postselected conversion~\cite{beverland}. Hence $n_{\min}\geq\max(d,\nu)$. For rational targets, the equality case of the canonical form of Ref.~\cite[Theorem~5.3]{beverland} raises the nullity bound to $\nu+1$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:analytic}). We did not find this stated, and Beverland \emph{et al.} use the automorphism $\sigma$ only to bound postselection probabilities~\cite[Proposition~5.8]{beverland}. It is, however, immediate from their results, so Table~\ref{tab:final} marks the $d=1$ entries $\nu+1$ with a dagger rather than as new. Their stabilizer-extent bound (e.g.\ $3.63$ for CCZ) is not established under arbitrary postselection, so we do not use it; compare the probability-weighted bounds of Ref.~\cite{seddon} below.

\paragraph*{Deterministic or high-probability protocols.}
The robustness bound of Regula and Takagi~\cite{regulatakagi}, $n\geq\log[(1-\epsilon)/F]/\log(4-2\sqrt2)$, is independent of $d$. It gives $n\geq4$ for CCZ ($F=9/16$), $n\geq3$ for CS ($F=5/8$) and $n\geq8$ for two CCZ states. Seddon \emph{et al.}~\cite{seddon} weight such bounds by the success probability $P$; for CCZ they give $n\geq4$ only when $P>0.83$.

\paragraph*{Class-restricted bounds.}
Codes with a transversal non-Clifford phase gate need at least fifteen qubits for distance three~\cite{kbk}. Generalized triorthogonal codes are classified through length 54 at distance at least three~\cite{nezamihaah,wjs}, and footprint bounds limit particular circuit families~\cite{jacinto}. None of these covers adaptive, non-transversal or multistage protocols. The optimal CS factory and the two-level designs lie outside all of these classes.
